Where Fulton Heights is

The National Register nomination places Fulton Heights about a mile south of the central business district, inside the City of Salisbury. The district is roughly bounded by Fulton Street, Heilig Avenue, Ridge Avenue and Boyden Street, covers about 122 acres and was listed in 1999.

It includes 439 contributing buildings, and the period of significance runs from 1903 to 1948. Mitchell Avenue is the main spine, and the nomination also names Maupin, Wiley, Ridge, Elm, Heilig and Stanley among the district's streets.

The streetcar line and the bungalows

The Salisbury and Spencer streetcar system ran from 1905 to 1938, and the nomination says its tracks reached Fulton Heights in 1906. The landscaped median on Mitchell Avenue was added around 1938 over those tracks.

A few late Victorian houses came before 1910, but most homes date from the 1910s and 1920s, when Craftsman bungalows dominated. Duplexes and small apartments appeared in the late 1920s and the 1940s, and Minimal Traditional houses followed after the war.

Lots, alleys and what they mean for outdoor plumbing

Typical lots are about fifty feet wide and 175 feet deep, and houses generally cover less than half of each lot. Original alleys still serve the rear of most houses, and granite or concrete retaining walls separate many lawns from the sidewalk.

On a deep lot the run from the street to the house is longer than on a short one, and the route a sewer line takes is not obvious from the house. A plumber who locates the line before digging protects your lawn and any retaining wall, and a camera inspection shows where a recurring problem sits.

Buying a Fulton Heights house? Plumbing checks worth asking for

These checks apply to a house of any age, and they give you facts instead of assumptions.

  • A camera look at the main drain, especially if the seller cannot say when it was last cleaned.
  • The age and condition of the water heater, plus the discharge pipe at its relief valve.
  • Working shutoff valves under sinks and at toilets.
  • Water pressure at a hose bib and at an upstairs fixture.
  • Any visible repairs, patches or stains under sinks and around the water heater.

What this means when you call a plumber

The history above does not tell you what is inside a particular wall, and this page does not claim to. What it does tell you is that most houses here are about a century old and sit on deep lots, so the useful steps are practical ones.

Shut off the water at the nearest valve, call, and describe what you see. Ask for a camera look at the drain and a pressure check before agreeing to a large repair, and ask for the scope and the price in plain terms before work begins.

Local rule to check

Is Fulton Heights a local historic district?

The city's list of local historic districts has five entries, and Fulton Heights is not one of them. The city also says National Register listing alone is mostly honorary and does not require a Certificate of Appropriateness for exterior work unless the property is also in a local district.

Rules can change and individual properties can be treated differently, so confirm your address with the city before exterior work.

Plumbing questions about Fulton Heights

A plumber can look at exposed pipe, run a camera through a drain and test pressure, and those steps show what is there and how it is performing. Pipe material cannot be guessed from the house's age, so ask what was actually found.

The City of Salisbury says the property owner arranges repairs to a private water service line after the utility shuts the water off at the meter. Ask Salisbury-Rowan Utilities where the connection at your meter ends.

Minor repairs that do not change pipe routing, size, fuel type or venting are generally treated differently from replacements and new work. The work follows the plumbing section of the North Carolina State Building Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the city can tell you which permit applies to your job.

Fulton Heights includes duplexes and small apartments. Call and describe the building, who owns it and what is wrong, and we will talk through the next step.

Ask for the license number first, then search it at nclicensing.org. State law requires a license for plumbing contracting done for pay.
