A short history that explains the pipes

East Spencer was once called Southern City and was renamed in 1901. It covers about 1.6 square miles and had a 2020 population of 1,567, so most homes sit close together on small lots.

Close lots mean shared fences and short runs between the house and the sewer main, and many homes date to the railroad era. Older houses often have older drain lines, which is why we pay attention to pipe material before choosing a repair.

East Spencer water: soft and low in minerals

East Spencer's published water quality report lists hardness near 23 ppm, with readings between 17 and 28, alkalinity around 25 and a pH of 7.1. That is soft water with few minerals.

Soft water leaves little scale, which is good for heaters and fixtures. It can also be less protective of older metal pipe, so we watch for corrosion and pinhole leaks in aging lines. If you see them often, leak detection and a repiping discussion are worth having.

Tracks, trains and how older lots affect plumbing work

Homes near the Southern Railway corridor sit on small lots, so a sewer lateral often runs under shared driveways or tight side yards. That changes how we dig or line a pipe, and we plan around fences and neighboring property lines.

We locate the line first, talk through the route with you and keep the work as contained as possible.

Keeping older East Spencer pipes healthy

Replace brittle supply lines under sinks, keep a few drops of water running in a freezing snap for pipes in exposed spaces and insulate lines in crawl spaces and attics. These are small steps that prevent many winter calls.

If your home has galvanized supply pipe, ask us for an honest read on how much life it has left.
