What repiping means and when it is worth it

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry water to your fixtures. It is a bigger project than a patch, and it is worth it when the pipes are failing across the house instead of at one spot.

A homeowner on a single leak should start with leak detection or burst pipe repair. When leaks keep appearing, the pipe itself is the problem.

Materials that often lead to repiping

Galvanized steel

Common in older homes, it corrodes inside, narrowing the pipe and discoloring water.

Polybutylene

A gray plastic used in many homes from the late 1970s into the 1990s with a record of failure at fittings.

Old copper with pinholes

Pitting leaks can appear across a run.

Mixed old patches

Several generations of repairs can be harder to trust.

What Salisbury's housing mix means for repiping

Salisbury includes Fulton Heights homes built between about 1903 and 1948, mid-century houses and newer subdivisions. The oldest homes may still carry galvanized supply lines, while houses from the late 1970s through the 1990s sometimes contain polybutylene.

Knowing the age of your home and the pipe material guides the conversation. We identify what is behind your walls before quoting.

Water quality and your pipes

Utilities publish water quality reports, and these are worth reading. Salisbury-Rowan Utilities reports lead below the action level in its most recent sampling, but older household plumbing and fixtures can still contribute metals at the tap.

If you are concerned, ask us about testing and about an ANSI-certified filter. Repiping an older home can remove old materials from the path the water takes.

What to do before the repipe starts

Preparation protects your belongings and shortens the job.

  • Clear access to the water heater, the main shutoff and the crawl space entry.
  • Move furniture away from walls where access openings may be needed.
  • Plan for a few hours without water on the days we tie in new lines.
  • Take photos of walls and ceilings before work begins.

Living through a repipe

Repiping disrupts the house, but it does not have to take it over. We plan the work in phases, protect floors and furniture, and tell you when water will be off so you can plan showers and laundry.

Most of the work happens in crawl spaces, attics and a few access openings. We aim to leave walls intact and to make any opening neat enough to patch easily.

Afterward you should notice steadier pressure, clearer water and no more surprise pinhole leaks. If any of that is missing, call us and we will come back.

Code check

Code and standards for repiping

Repiping is regulated work. The North Carolina plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), sets approved materials, sizing, supports and testing, and inspections confirm them.

Pressure testing before the walls close is a standard step.
