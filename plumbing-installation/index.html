What plumbing installation covers

New plumbing is more than hooking up fixtures. It includes supply lines to each fixture, drain and vent piping that carries waste away and lets air in, shutoff valves and connections to the main supply and sewer.

We work with homeowners, builders and remodelers. If a problem has already happened, our leak detection and repair pages cover that side.

Permits, inspections and historic districts in Salisbury

Plumbing work generally must follow the plumbing section of the North Carolina State Building Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the City of Salisbury Development Services office at 132 N. Main St issues permits. Inspections happen at rough-in and final stages.

Salisbury has five local historic districts, and the Historic Preservation Commission requires a Certificate of Appropriateness for exterior changes there. A new vent, meter location or visible exterior line may need that approval, so we ask about it at the start.

Planning an installation so it goes smoothly

Decide on fixtures before rough-in, because their rough-in dimensions determine pipe locations. Changing a toilet or shower after the walls are closed can be expensive.

Tell us about future plans too. Adding a stub for a future bathroom while walls are open costs much less than opening them again later. For a heater, see water heater installation.

Scheduling around a remodel

Plumbing happens in two visits for most remodels. Rough-in comes before drywall, and finish comes after cabinets and tile. Tell us your schedule and we will line up with your other trades.

A delay in one trade ripples through the rest, so confirm dates early and tell us when anything shifts.

Fixtures, shutoffs and the details that keep a new install trouble-free

Every fixture we install gets its own shutoff valve, so a future repair does not mean turning off the whole house. We use braided supply lines, sized drain traps and properly vented drains.

Venting is easy to overlook and hard to fix later. A poorly vented drain gurgles, drains slowly and can pull water out of traps, letting sewer gas into the room.

We also test everything under pressure before closing walls and again after the fixtures are set. Catching a leak in an open wall costs minutes. Catching it after the drywall is up costs a day.

Water softeners and filtration systems

Softeners, whole-house filters and under-sink filtration all tie into your supply lines. They also need drain connections, bypass valves and a location a service technician can reach later.

East Spencer's water measures about 23 ppm hardness, which is soft, so many homes in the area do not need a softener at all. Filtration for taste or sediment is the more common request, and if a softener you already own has stopped working we can check the bypass, the drain line and the fittings.

New construction, additions and rough-in

Rough-in is the pipe work set inside walls and under slabs before they are closed up. On new construction and additions we coordinate with the builder so supply lines, drains and vents are placed correctly and inspected before drywall goes up.

Planning the fixture locations early avoids moving pipe later. Bring your drawings or a rough sketch to the first call and we can talk through the layout.

Gas piping: if you smell gas, leave first

Gas piping for appliances such as water heaters and ranges is part of many installation projects, and it is held to a stricter standard than water lines. A suspected gas leak is not a plumbing call to place first.

  • Leave the building and take everyone with you.
  • Do not use switches, phones or flames inside.
  • Call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Call us once the building is safe and cleared for work.
