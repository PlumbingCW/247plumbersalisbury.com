Local plumbers

Built around how Salisbury houses and water actually behave

Salisbury plumbing is a mix of eras. Fulton Heights got its streetcar line in 1906, and its Colonial Revival and Bungalow homes were built between about 1903 and 1948, which means many have decades of repairs inside the walls. If your house is in that age range, an emergency leak often turns out to involve older supply lines or drain pipe, and a plumber who has seen them before saves time.

Downtown and the surrounding local historic districts add another layer. The Salisbury Historic Preservation Commission requires a Certificate of Appropriateness for exterior changes in the local districts, so a vent, a meter relocation or a visible line outside should be checked before it is installed. We plan around that rather than discovering it halfway through the job.

Water in Salisbury comes from Salisbury-Rowan Utilities, which draws from the Yadkin River and also supplies Spencer, East Spencer, Granite Quarry, China Grove and Landis. East Spencer’s published water report lists hardness near 23 ppm, which is soft, so heavy scale is less of a concern than in many parts of the country. Soft water does not remove other problems like corroded fittings or aging heaters, but it does make tankless maintenance simpler.
