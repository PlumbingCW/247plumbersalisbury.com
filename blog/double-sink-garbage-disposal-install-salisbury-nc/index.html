How to Install a Double Sink With Garbage Disposal in Salisbury, NC

A double sink with a disposal looks simple, but the drain has to be assembled in the right order and the right slope. Getting it wrong is how kitchens end up with slow sinks and leaks under the cabinet.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 17, 2026.6 min read.

How the drain fits together

Water leaves each basin through a basket strainer or the disposal flange. A continuous waste tube joins the two basins, and the combined flow goes through the disposal, then the trap, then into the wall drain.

The disposal usually sits under one basin, and the other basin drains through a tailpiece and a tee into the disposal outlet or into the trap arm. Which layout you use depends on your cabinet and the position of the wall drain. For repairs and installs on a disposal itself, see our garbage disposal page.

Order of work

Set the sink and strainers

Seal the basket strainers and drain flange with plumber's putty.

Mount the disposal

Attach the mounting assembly and hang the unit.

Connect the drain

Join the second basin, the trap and the wall drain with proper slope.

Connect the dishwasher

Run the dishwasher drain through a high loop or air gap to the disposal.

Measuring before you buy

Check the depth of the sink basins and the space beneath them. A deep basin with a disposal and a trap can run out of cabinet room fast, especially in older kitchens with shallow base cabinets.

Measure from the bottom of the flange to the cabinet floor, then compare it to the disposal's height and the trap's drop. If the numbers are tight, a shorter disposal or a different trap layout may be needed.

Mistakes that cause trouble later

Trap too low or too high

The trap must sit within the allowable distance below the vent.

Dishwasher without a high loop

Dirty water can flow back into the dishwasher.

Overtightened plastic fittings

Cracks appear weeks later.

Unsupported disposal

A loose mount vibrates and leaks.

Common miss

Dishwashers and disposals share a drain

The dishwasher drain hose usually connects to the disposal inlet or to the sink drain tailpiece. Remove the knockout plug inside the disposal inlet when you connect it, or the dishwasher will not drain.

That small plug has caused more than one flooded dishwasher call.

Testing the finished installation

Fill both basins and release the water at once, watching every joint. Then run the disposal with cold water and the dishwasher through a short cycle.

Leaks often appear only under load, so check again after fifteen minutes with a dry paper towel under each connection.

Before you start

Check these first.

  • Is there a switch or outlet for the disposal?
  • Does the cabinet have room for the trap?
  • Where does the dishwasher hose connect?
  • Does the wall drain sit at the right height?
  • Do you have a bucket and towels?

What the local rules and utility mean for your kitchen

Kitchen sink drains in Salisbury connect to the public sewer run by Salisbury-Rowan Utilities. Fats poured down a sink can build up in lines on both sides of the property line, and the utility's sewer has to carry whatever you send down.

Plumbing work done for pay in North Carolina requires a license under G.S. 87-21, with exemptions for minor repairs that do not change routing, size, fuel type or venting. A like-for-like disposal replacement is a minor job, while rearranging the drain is not.

What we see in older Salisbury kitchens

Kitchens in homes from the early twentieth century often have old galvanized or cast iron drains that are rough inside. A new sink with a powerful disposal can push more debris into those lines than they were built to carry.

In East Spencer and Spencer, we check the wall drain when we install a disposal and tell you if the line behind it needs attention.

When to replace the whole drain assembly

If the old tailpieces are corroded, thin or cracked, replace them with the disposal. Mixing a new disposal with old fittings is a common cause of leaks that appear weeks later.

The cost of a new set of fittings is small compared with a cabinet floor that has been quietly soaking.

Noise, vibration and mounting

A disposal is a motor, and it hums and vibrates against the sink. Quality mounts with rubber isolation and a sound shield make a real difference in a kitchen that opens to a living space.

If noise bothered you with the old unit, mention it when you choose a new one. Heavier motors with extra insulation run quieter.

Keeping the system clear

Run cold water while the disposal runs and for several seconds afterward. Skip grease, large bones and fibrous scraps like celery or corn husks.

If the sink still drains slowly after installation, the clog is probably downstream, and drain cleaning is the next step.

Code check

Codes and standards worth knowing

Trap, vent and dishwasher connection rules come from the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in North Carolina, and disposal wiring falls under the National Electrical Code (NEC).
