Do You Need a Permit to Install a Water Heater in Salisbury, NC?

For most water heater replacements, the answer is yes. A permit is the City's way of making sure the venting, gas or electrical connection, relief valve and drain are done to code.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 20, 2026.6 min read.

Short answer and what to do next

Water heater work falls under the plumbing section of the North Carolina State Building Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and the City of Salisbury Development Services office issues permits for plumbing systems. The office is at 132 N. Main St, open Monday through Friday, 8:30 to 5, and takes calls at (704) 638-5208.

If a licensed plumber does the work, they normally pull the permit and schedule the inspection. If you replace the heater yourself in your own home, confirm what the office requires before you start. Our water heater installation page explains what a planned install includes.

What an inspector usually looks at

Relief valve and discharge pipe

The valve must be present and drain to a safe location.

Venting or electrical

Gas units need proper vents. Electric units need correct wiring.

Shutoff valves and connections

Water lines must be properly supported and connected.

Drain pan or protection

Some locations need a pan to protect floors.

Expansion control

Closed systems may need an expansion tank.

What to have ready before you call the permit office

Know the address, the type of heater, the fuel and whether the unit is moving or staying in place. If a plumber is doing the work, have the contractor's name and license information available.

These details help staff tell you whether a permit is required and what an inspector will check.

How the permit process runs

Contact the office

Confirm the permit requirements and fees.

Submit details

Provide the address, contractor and scope.

Install

Complete the work to code.

Inspection

An inspector checks the installation and signs off.

Plan ahead

What happens on inspection day

The inspector looks at the visible parts of the installation, such as the vent, the relief valve discharge, the supply connections and the electrical or gas feed. They may also test the relief valve and check the drain pan.

If something does not meet the code, you get a list of corrections and a chance to fix and recheck. Plan for the inspection window rather than for an exact time.

Replacing a heater in a rental or an inherited home

Landlords and heirs often replace heaters in a hurry. Pulling the permit protects the owner and the next buyer, and it ensures the unit is safe for tenants.

If the work is done by a licensed plumber, the permit is part of the job. Ask to see the permit number and keep the inspection record with the house papers.

Licensing and what the state says

North Carolina requires a license for anyone who engages in plumbing contracting for pay, under G.S. 87-21. The State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating, and Fire Sprinkler Contractors oversees licensing, and its License Search at nclicensing.org lets you confirm a contractor's license.

Minor repairs and replacements that do not change pipe routing, size, fuel type or venting are treated differently, which is why a like-for-like swap is sometimes simpler than a move or a fuel change. The City's office is the right place to confirm which side of the line your job falls on.

Historic districts and exterior work

Salisbury has five local historic districts, including West Square, Downtown, Brooklyn-South Square, North Main Street and Ellis Street Graded School. The Historic Preservation Commission requires a Certificate of Appropriateness for exterior changes there.

A new vent through a visible wall or a tankless unit mounted on the street side can count as an exterior change. Ask before you install, and mention it to your plumber.

Permits and your homeowners insurance

Insurers sometimes ask whether major work in the house was permitted when they review a claim. A documented permit and inspection show that the installation met the code at the time.

Keep the permit, the inspection sign-off and the invoice together. They also help at resale, when a buyer's inspector looks at the water heater.

A short checklist for permit day

Bring the address, the unit details, the contractor's information and a sketch of the location if you have one. Ask about fees, the inspection window and how to reach the inspector.

Write down the permit number and keep it with the receipt. If anything changes on site, such as moving the heater, tell the office before work continues.

Why skipping the permit costs more later

An unpermitted installation can complicate a home sale, an insurance claim or an inspection. A buyer's inspector may ask for proof the work was permitted, and the fix is usually harder after the walls are closed.

If you are replacing an old heater in Granite Quarry or China Grove, the same rules apply through the plumbing code. For an urgent failure, see water heater replacement.
