Sewer Line Leak Detection: A Salisbury, NC Homeowner Guide

Sewer leaks are quiet. Water and waste escape into the soil, and the first signs are a smell, a green patch in the yard or a drain that keeps slowing down.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 2, 2026.6 min read.

How to know if a sewer line is leaking

A leaking sewer line usually shows up as a persistent sewer odor outside, a soggy or unusually green patch above the line, slow drains throughout the house or a sunken area in the lawn. A single slow sink is rarely a sewer line problem. Several fixtures behaving badly at once is.

The only way to confirm a leak is to look inside the pipe. A plumber runs a camera through the line, then uses a locator to mark where any defect sits and how deep it is. Our sewer camera inspection service does exactly that.

Signs worth acting on

Odor in the yard

Sewage smell near one spot suggests waste is reaching the soil.

Lush patch of grass

Waste water fertilizes the grass above a break.

Gurgling in several drains

Air and water are fighting through a restricted line.

Mold or damp smell in a crawl space

Leaks near the house can wet the soil under it.

Rodents or insects near the foundation

Broken lines give pests a path.

Sinkholes or soft ground

Soil washes into a cracked pipe.

What a camera inspection costs you in time, not just money

A typical inspection takes about an hour once the plumber has access to the line. You do not need to be home for the whole visit, but being there for the last ten minutes lets the plumber show you the footage while it is fresh.

Ask for the recording and a short written note on what was seen, where and how deep. That record is useful if you ever sell the house, file an insurance claim or compare quotes from two companies.

Why sewer lines leak in older neighborhoods

Most sewer leaks come from joints that opened, pipe that cracked or roots that pushed in. Clay pipe has many joints, and cast iron corrodes from the inside. Plastic pipe rarely rots, but it can sag or separate if soil shifts.

Mature trees along older streets make roots a common cause. A fine root finds the smallest gap in a joint and grows into a mat that eventually catches paper and slows the flow.

Good to know

A word on dye tests and smoke tests

Some plumbers add a dye test to see where water goes, or a smoke test to find vent and drain leaks above ground. These are useful for specific questions, like a sewer smell inside the house, but they do not replace a camera for a buried line.

If a company suggests digging before it has looked inside the pipe, ask why. Most sewer problems can be seen first and dug second.

Preventing the next leak

Keep tree roots in mind when you plant. Large trees over the lateral are a long-term risk, and a camera check every few years is cheap insurance for an older line.

Avoid flushing wipes and grease, and do not drive heavy equipment over the lateral. Small habits protect a pipe you cannot see.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A little prep makes the search faster.

  • Find your cleanout, often a capped pipe near the foundation.
  • Note where odors or wet spots appear.
  • Stop watering the area so wet soil does not hide the leak.
  • Gather any records of past sewer work.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaners into the drains.

Where this matters most around Salisbury

Older homes in Fulton Heights, built between about 1903 and 1948, and in Spencer are the most likely to have clay or cast iron laterals. Granite Quarry homes may sit on rockier ground that affects repair methods, and our Granite Quarry page explains how.

Newer subdivisions usually have PVC or ABS pipe, where problems tend to be sagging or poor joints. A camera tells you which situation you have, and it is far cheaper than guessing.

Signs that the leak is on the street side instead

If the camera shows a clean lateral all the way to the main and the problem appears near the street, the defect may be in the public main or at the tap. In that case, the utility owns the repair, and your footage is the best evidence you can give them.

Call Salisbury-Rowan Utilities customer service, describe what the camera showed and give them the distance from your cleanout. A clear report gets a crew to the right spot faster.

What happens after the leak is found

A small defect may only need a sewer line repair at one spot. If a long run is cracked, lining the pipe from the inside can avoid digging up the yard. A collapsed section needs excavation.

Ask for the recording, the depth of the defect and the price of each method. A good plumber gives all three without pressure.
