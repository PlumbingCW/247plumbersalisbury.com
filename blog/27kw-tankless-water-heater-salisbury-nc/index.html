27 kW Tankless Water Heater in Salisbury, NC: What to Know

A 27 kW electric tankless heater can supply a lot of hot water at once, but it also asks a lot of your electrical system. The wiring is usually the deciding factor, not the heater.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 15, 2026.6 min read.

How much power it actually needs

Divide 27,000 watts by 240 volts and you get about 112 amps. That is more than many entire homes have available as spare capacity, and it means large breakers and heavy wire, often several of each.

Before you shop for a unit, have someone look at your electrical panel and service size. If the panel cannot carry the load, the cost of an upgrade can exceed the cost of the heater. Our tankless water heater page explains the broader tradeoffs.

What a 27 kW unit asks of your house

Electrical service

Often 200 amps or more with space in the panel.

Breakers and wire

Multiple double-pole breakers and heavy conductors.

Cold water temperature

Colder incoming water reduces the flow you get.

Flow demand

Two showers and a dishwasher together can stretch it.

The cost of electrical upgrades, in plain terms

A panel that is already full needs more than a few extra breakers. Upgrading the service, running new heavy wire and adding a sub-panel can cost more than the heater, and it adds permit and inspection steps.

Ask for the electrical estimate in writing before you buy the unit. That number often decides whether tankless is the right idea for your house.

Gas, heat pump or electric tankless

A gas tankless heater trades electrical demand for a gas line and a vent. A heat pump tank trades speed for efficiency. An electric tankless unit is simple and compact, but it asks the most of your panel.

None is best for everyone. We compare the three for your house and give you the numbers side by side.

Why Yadkin River water matters

Salisbury-Rowan Utilities draws from the Yadkin River, a surface source, and surface water cools off in winter. The colder the incoming water, the more heat the unit must add, and the lower the flow it can keep up.

A winter test is the honest measure. A unit that works fine in October may feel tight in January, and a plumber who sizes for winter avoids that surprise.

A realistic test of hot water demand

Count the fixtures that run together at your peak time, such as two showers, a dishwasher and a washing machine. Then estimate the flow each uses and add them up.

If the total exceeds what the unit can heat in winter, you will notice it on the coldest mornings. That is the moment to size up, add a second unit or choose a different type of heater.

Soft water helps electric tankless heaters

East Spencer's published water report lists hardness near 23 ppm, which is soft. Scale on electric heating elements is the main maintenance issue for tankless units, and soft water slows that buildup.

Salisbury, Spencer, Granite Quarry, China Grove and Landis all receive water through the same utility, so your local report is the best starting point. Plumbing work for a water heater is regulated under the North Carolina State Building Code, and the City of Salisbury Development Services office handles permits.

Questions to ask before buying

These save money and surprises.

  • What is my panel capacity today?
  • Does the unit have multiple heating chambers that can be sized by flow?
  • What is the minimum incoming temperature in my coldest week?
  • Who will upgrade the electrical and pull permits?
  • How will I flush it?

When a smaller or gas unit makes more sense

If your panel is small, a smaller electric unit, a gas tankless heater or a heat pump tank may suit better. Electric tankless units are simple and compact, but a heavy draw is a real cost in both installation and use.

We compare options in writing, including the cost of electrical work. See water heater installation for a planned project and China Grove or Spencer if you want to know whether we cover your address.

Maintenance you will actually do

A tankless heater asks for a flush now and then and a clean inlet screen. With soft water, the schedule can be longer, but it should still exist.

Put the date on the unit and a reminder in your phone. A heater that is flushed on schedule keeps its flow rating, and one that is ignored slowly loses it.

What installation looks like

A licensed electrician handles the circuit work, and a plumber sets the unit, isolation valves and connections. We coordinate the visit so both pieces meet the permit and inspection.

After installation we run the unit, test flow and temperature and show you how to flush it. A short walkthrough means you know what normal looks like.

Code check

Codes and standards worth knowing

Electrical work for a unit this size follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), and the plumbing side follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in North Carolina.
