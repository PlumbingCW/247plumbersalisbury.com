PEX Plumbing Pipe Installation: A Salisbury, NC Homeowner Guide

PEX is flexible plastic pipe that has become a common choice for supply lines. It bends around corners, uses fewer fittings and resists freezing damage better than rigid pipe, which is why plumbers like it for retrofits.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 26, 2026.6 min read.

Why PEX is popular

PEX is lightweight and can be pulled through walls and crawl spaces with fewer joints than copper. Fewer joints mean fewer places for a leak to start.

It does have limits. It should be protected from direct sunlight, and some chemicals can harm it. For a house with failing supply lines, it often makes a repiping project faster and less invasive.

Where PEX works well

Whole house repiping

Long runs without many fittings.

Additions

Specialized applications.

Fittings, tools and how joints are made

PEX joints are made with crimp rings, cinch clamps, expansion rings or push-fit connectors. Each needs its own tool or fitting system, and the parts are not interchangeable.

A professional chooses one system and sticks with it for the whole job. Mixing parts is one of the common causes of slow leaks at PEX joints.

Layout choice

Manifolds make shutoffs easy

A manifold is a central distribution point with a shutoff for each fixture line. If you need to work on one bathroom, you close one valve and keep the rest of the house running.

It uses more tubing than a branch layout, but the convenience is real and so is the pressure stability when two fixtures run at once.

Protecting PEX where it matters

Keep PEX out of direct sunlight, away from open flame and from contact with certain petroleum products. Use sleeves where it passes through framing, and support it at the intervals the maker specifies.

In a crawl space, insulate it against cold and keep it off the ground. In an attic, keep it clear of recessed light housings and flues.

Code, permits and cold snaps

PEX is widely accepted under plumbing codes, and North Carolina's State Building Code includes a plumbing section that governs materials and installation. The City of Salisbury Development Services office issues permits for plumbing systems, and inspections confirm supports, fittings and pressure tests.

Winter cold snaps catch crawl space pipes in the Piedmont now and then. PEX tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe, though a frozen line should still be protected and checked.

PEX in older Salisbury homes

Fulton Heights and other neighborhoods built between about 1903 and 1948 often have galvanized or aging copper supply pipe. PEX can replace it with fewer walls opened because the tubing snakes through existing chases.

In Spencer and East Spencer, where soft water can be less kind to older metal pipe, PEX avoids that issue altogether, since plastic does not corrode.

Questions to ask a plumber about PEX

Good answers show a careful installer.

  • Which fitting system do you use and why?
  • How will the tubing be supported?
  • Will you use a manifold?
  • How is the tubing protected from sunlight?
  • Will you pressure test and keep the results?

What to do with leftover old pipe

After a repipe, the old galvanized or copper pipe may remain in place inside walls and floors. It is usually capped and left, because removing it costs more than it saves.

Ask the plumber to cap and label what remains and to note it on a sketch. A future owner will thank you when they open a wall.

Costs and time, in general terms

PEX materials cost less than copper, and the labor is faster because there are fewer joints and no soldering. The savings show up on larger projects, where the difference in time adds up.

Access to the pipe path is still the main cost driver. A crawl space makes the work simple, while finished walls and ceilings add patching and paint.

Ask for the price split into material, labor and restoration. That shows what you are paying for and where you can save.

Signs your PEX job was done well

Tubing runs with gentle bends and no kinks, with supports at regular spacing. Fittings are consistent, and every connection is visible at the manifold or the fixture.

The plumber should leave you a pressure test result and a labeled shutoff for each branch. If any of that is missing, ask for it before you pay.

Doing it yourself versus hiring

PEX is friendlier to do-it-yourself work than copper, but a whole-house project is still a lot of tubing, fittings and testing. Mistakes show up as leaks inside walls.

For small additions, a competent homeowner may manage. For a full repipe or a project that needs a permit, a licensed plumber is the safer choice, and our plumbing installation page describes how we plan one.

Code check

Codes and standards worth knowing

PEX tubing and fittings must carry the standards markings required by the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in North Carolina, and installers follow the manufacturer's instructions.
