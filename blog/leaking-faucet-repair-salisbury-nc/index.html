Leaking Faucet Repair in Salisbury, NC: A Homeowner Guide

A dripping faucet wastes water all day and gets worse over time. Most of the time the fix is a small part, and knowing which part saves you a trip to the hardware store.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published October 5, 2026.6 min read.

Start by finding out where the leak is

A faucet leaks in one of three places. Water drips from the spout, pools at the base or handle, or collects under the sink. Each location points to a different part.

A spout drip means a worn cartridge, washer or valve seat. A leak at the base means worn seals or a loose mounting. A leak under the sink usually means a supply line or connection, and if that is happening, our faucet and sink repair page covers the fix.

What each kind of leak usually means

Drip from the spout

The internal seal no longer shuts off the water.

Leak around the handle

Seals or packing nuts have worn.

Pool at the base

O-rings or the mounting are loose.

Wet cabinet floor

A supply line, shutoff valve or drain is leaking.

Spray in the wrong direction

A clogged aerator or damaged spray head.

What a drip actually costs

A faucet that drips once a second wastes thousands of gallons over a year. On a hot water tap, you also pay to heat the water that disappears down the drain.

Even if you are not sure what is wrong, closing the angle stops under the sink while you decide stops the loss at no cost.

A basic repair sequence

Shut off the water

Close the small angle stops under the sink.

Identify the faucet type

Cartridge, ball, ceramic disc and compression faucets differ.

Replace the failed part

Match the cartridge or seal to the brand.

Test for leaks

Turn the water on slowly and check all connections.

Worth knowing

Kitchen faucets and bathroom faucets age differently

A kitchen faucet takes more abuse, with heavier handle use and a pull-out hose that wears. Bathroom faucets run less but live with soap and toothpaste that build up on the cartridge.

When one leaks, check the other. They were often installed together and are about the same age.

Three mistakes that turn a small leak into a bigger job

The first is overtightening. Cranking a packing nut or a handle screw can crack a plastic part or strip the threads.

The second is using the wrong cartridge. Many look alike and fit badly. Take the old one to the store or photograph the brand name.

The third is waiting. A slow leak under the sink can rot a cabinet floor before anyone notices, and the repair then involves carpentry as well as plumbing.

When a replacement makes more sense

A faucet that is corroded, pitted or discontinued is not worth repairing. If the parts cost approaches the price of a new faucet, replace it. A new faucet also gives you a chance to switch to a model with a better cartridge and a finish that resists spotting.

Repair is the right choice for a faucet in good shape that has a single failing part. A plumber can usually tell in a couple of minutes.

Salisbury water and your faucet

Salisbury-Rowan Utilities draws water from the Yadkin River. East Spencer's published water report, which covers water from the same regional system, lists hardness near 23 ppm, which is soft. Soft water leaves less scale on aerators and cartridges than hard water does, so faucets here tend to last longer between repairs.

That is good news, but it does not stop rubber seals from aging. If your faucet is more than ten or twelve years old and starts dripping, the seals have probably just worn out.

Parts and tools to have ready

These items cover most faucet repairs.

  • Adjustable wrench and a screwdriver set.
  • A hex key set for handle screws.
  • Replacement cartridge or washers matched to your brand.
  • Plumber's grease for O-rings.
  • A towel and a small bowl for the parts.

Renters and landlords: who fixes the drip

In a rental, a leaking faucet is usually the landlord's responsibility, and reporting it in writing protects you. A drip left alone can turn into a bigger claim if water damages a cabinet.

If you own the home, set a reminder to check under each sink twice a year. A flashlight and a dry paper towel find slow leaks long before they ruin a cabinet floor.

What to ask for if you hire a plumber

Ask the plumber to replace the supply lines and test the angle stops while the faucet is open, especially in homes from the early twentieth century in neighborhoods like Fulton Heights, where old shutoffs are common. Ask what warranty applies to the repair.

In East Spencer and Landis, where many homes are older, tell the plumber the age of the faucet so they bring parts that fit.

Code check

Codes and standards worth knowing

Replacement faucets and supply lines should carry ANSI/NSF certification, and installation follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in North Carolina.
