Sewer Hydro Jetting Cost: What Salisbury, NC Homeowners Should Know

There is no single price for hydro jetting a sewer line, and anyone who quotes one without seeing the line is guessing. The real price depends on a handful of factors you can check yourself.

By the Salisbury 24/7 Plumbing team.Published November 1, 2026.6 min read.

What you are paying for

A hydro jetting visit pays for a trained operator, a truck-mounted or portable jetter, a hose and nozzle matched to your pipe, access to the line and the time to make several passes. Many plumbers include a camera look before and after.

The price is higher than a basic cable clearing because the equipment and skill are greater. It is also the method that actually cleans the pipe wall, which is why it can be worth the difference for the right situation. See our hydro jetting page for how the process works.

Factors that move the price

Line length and size

A long four-inch line takes more time than a short one.

Access

A cleanout makes jetting simple. No cleanout may mean pulling a toilet.

Severity

Heavy grease, scale or roots take more passes.

Camera inspection

We recommend one before jetting any older pipe.

Timing

After-hours emergency calls are priced differently.

Follow-up repairs

If the camera finds a break, jetting alone will not help.

What a quote should include

A good quote names the line being jetted, the access point, the method and whether a camera inspection is included. It should also say what happens if the camera shows damage.

Be careful with a quote that gives only a flat number by phone for a main line. Without seeing the line, the number is a guess, and the surprise often shows up at the end.

Jetting compared with other options

A cheaper method can cost more if the clog returns.

MethodUpfrontLasting result
Cable clearingLowerOften shorter, leaves coating.
Hydro jettingHigherCleans walls, lasts longer in sound pipe.
Repair or liningHighestFixes the pipe itself.
Worth asking

Camera before and after

A camera pass before jetting shows pipe condition and where the buildup is. A pass afterward shows the result. Together they tell you whether the money was well spent.

Ask for both recordings, and keep them with your home records.

How to compare quotes fairly

Ask every company the same questions.

  • Is a camera inspection included before jetting?
  • What pipe size and length will you jet?
  • How many passes and how long does it take?
  • What happens if the camera shows a break?
  • Is the price firm before work starts?

Questions about timing and mess

Jetting is a wet, loud job at the cleanout, and some water and debris come back out of the opening. A good crew lays down protection and cleans up as they go.

Plan for a couple of hours at most for a typical home line. Longer or more complex lines take more time, and the plumber should tell you in advance.

Why the pipe matters in Salisbury

Many homes in Salisbury and nearby towns were built before the 1950s, and Fulton Heights alone has 439 contributing buildings from about 1903 to 1948. Houses of that age commonly have clay or cast iron sewer laterals that may be rough, rooted or fragile.

Salisbury-Rowan Utilities operates the public main, and the owner is generally responsible for the lateral. Jetting a fragile older line without a camera first can turn a clog into a break, so a reputable plumber looks first.

When jetting is not worth it

If the camera shows a collapsed pipe, a broken joint or a bellied section holding water, jetting only moves the problem. A sewer line repair is the honest answer.

If the clog was a one-time event, such as a flushed item, a simple cable clearing is cheaper and enough.

How to decide if the price is fair

Compare the scope, not just the number. A lower price that skips the camera and the cleanout access may cost more later when the clog returns.

A fair price comes with a clear description of the line, the method and what happens if the camera finds damage. If any of that is missing, ask before you agree.

A note on repeat jetting

If you pay for jetting and the line clogs again within months, something other than buildup is happening. A bellied pipe, an offset joint or a root mass in a joint will keep catching debris.

At that point, the better spend is on finding and fixing the cause rather than paying for another cleaning. A camera look usually costs less than two cleanings, and it gives you a plan instead of a guess.

Frequency and maintenance

Most homes never need jetting. Restaurants, apartment buildings and homes with chronic grease or root problems may benefit from a scheduled visit.

In Granite Quarry and East Spencer, we see mature trees and older laterals, and a camera visit after jetting gives you proof the line is clear and a baseline for later.

Code check

Codes and standards worth knowing

Cleanout access follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in North Carolina, and jetting equipment ratings set the safe pressure for a given pipe.
