A backup that is rising: what to do while you wait

When several fixtures back up at once, or sewage appears in a tub or floor drain, the blockage is probably in the main line. Stop running water, because every flush and shower adds to the overflow.

Call (704) 951-1635 and tell us which fixtures are affected. That helps us bring the right equipment for a main line clog.

  • Stop using sinks, tubs, showers and toilets.
  • Keep children and pets out of any standing water.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaners, because they can burn skin and harm pipes.
  • Open a window if there is a sewer smell.

How we clear different drains

Kitchen drains often hold grease and food. Bathroom drains hold hair and soap. Main lines can hold roots, flushed wipes or a bellied pipe where waste collects.

We start with the lightest tool that will work, usually a cable machine sized to the pipe. For tough buildup or grease we move to hydro jetting, and if the same clog keeps coming back we suggest a camera inspection to see what is actually in the pipe.

Older Salisbury pipes behave differently

Neighborhoods like Fulton Heights were built between about 1903 and 1948, and many homes from that era had cast iron or clay drain lines. Cast iron roughens inside as it ages, and clay joints invite roots, which is why repeat clogs are common in older homes with mature trees nearby.

Knowing the pipe material changes how we clean it. We match cable size and technique so we clear the clog without damaging an older line.

Keeping drains clear after we leave

Most clogs are habits in disguise. Grease poured down a kitchen sink cools and coats the pipe, hair collects in shower traps, and wipes sold as flushable rarely break down the way toilet paper does.

A drain strainer in each tub and kitchen sink, a bin for cooking fat and a rule that only toilet paper goes in the toilet prevent most repeat calls. If drains still slow down after that, there is likely a pipe issue worth looking at with a camera.

Kitchen sink clogs and grease buildup

Kitchen sinks clog differently from bathroom drains. Grease cools inside the pipe, food particles stick to it, and the line narrows until water pools in the basin.

A sink that drains slowly on one side usually has buildup in the trap or the branch line. Snaking clears the blockage, and if the grease keeps coming back, a camera look or hydro jetting shows how much of the pipe is coated. Hot water rarely clears grease that has already hardened farther down the line.

Shower and tub clogs, and drain lines that leak

Hair and soap film are the usual cause of a shower or tub that drains slowly. A drain that clears but then stains a ceiling below is a different problem, because it points to a leaking drain line instead of a clog.

We clear the blockage first, then run water to see whether the line holds. If it leaks, we explain the repair before any wall or ceiling is opened.

Code check

Code and standards for drain cleaning

Drain and vent rules in North Carolina follow the plumbing section of the State Building Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Clearing a clog is generally treated as maintenance, but adding a cleanout or changing a drain is regulated work.
