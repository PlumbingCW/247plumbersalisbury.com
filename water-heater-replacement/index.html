Signs the tank is done and replacement beats repair

A repair makes sense when the failure is a thermostat, element or valve. These four signs point to the tank itself.

Water at the base

Moisture under the tank usually means the steel shell has corroded through. A shell leak cannot be patched, so the unit has to come out.

Rust-colored hot water

Rusty water from hot taps only, with clean cold water, points to the tank interior breaking down.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment hardens on the bottom and the burner or element has to work through it. This wastes energy and shortens tank life.

A unit past its expected life

Tank heaters commonly run about eight to twelve years. Past that range, a repair often buys only a short extension.

Why Salisbury water heaters often last longer than the average

Water from Salisbury-Rowan Utilities is soft. East Spencer's published water quality report, which covers water supplied through the same utility system, lists hardness near 23 ppm. Soft water builds far less scale inside a tank than hard water does, so sediment and element burnout are less common here than in hard-water regions.

That does not make tanks immortal. The anode rod still sacrifices itself over the years, and once it is gone the steel starts to corrode. If your heater is more than a decade old and was never serviced, assume the anode is spent and plan the replacement before the leak, not after.

What a replacement visit includes

We confirm the fuel type, measure the space and check the venting or electrical supply. Then we choose a replacement that matches your household's hot water use, not just the size of the old tank.

New shutoff valves, a fresh temperature and pressure relief valve and discharge pipe, and updated connections are part of a proper installation. In Salisbury, a replacement is usually a permitted job, and the City's Development Services office at 132 N. Main St handles those permits. If you want to compare a tank swap against a different technology, the tankless water heater page explains the tradeoffs.

Before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of prep makes the visit faster.

  • Clear a three-foot path to the heater.
  • Find the main shutoff and the breaker or gas valve.
  • Note the age, size and fuel type printed on the label.
  • Take a photo of any leak for your records.

How to extend the life of the heater we install

A new tank lasts longest when someone looks at it once a year. Draining a few gallons clears sediment, testing the relief valve confirms it still opens, and checking the anode rod catches the slow loss that eventually lets rust start.

Keep the thermostat at a sensible setting and leave room around the unit. If you ever hear a new noise or see a damp spot, call early, because small issues are cheap and big ones are not.
