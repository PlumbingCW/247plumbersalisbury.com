Signs of a sewer line break that need quick action

A broken sewer line can send waste into your house or into the soil around it. Either one is a health issue as well as a plumbing problem.

If you see sewage in tubs or floor drains, stop all water use and call (704) 951-1635. We schedule a plumber as soon as availability allows.

  • Do not flush or run water until the line is cleared.
  • Keep children and pets away from backups and wet yard areas.
  • Wear gloves and avoid contact with contaminated water.

Common causes of sewer line failure in Rowan County homes

Tree roots

Roots work into joints and gradually crush or split older pipe.

Old clay or cast iron

Aged materials crack, corrode or separate.

Bellies or sags

Soil movement leaves a low spot where waste settles.

Ground disturbance

Construction and heavy loads can shift buried pipe.

Where your responsibility ends and the utility's begins

In most systems the property owner is responsible for the sewer lateral that runs from the house to the public main. Salisbury-Rowan Utilities operates the public system and can confirm where the dividing point sits for your address.

We diagnose the problem on your side of that line and let you know when a failure looks like it sits on the utility's side, so you can contact SRU customer service with specifics.

What happens after the repair is done

A repaired line should be inspected, not assumed. We run the camera through the repaired section to confirm the flow is smooth and the joints are tight, and we keep the recording for you.

If excavation was needed, we explain what restoration is left, such as grading, sod or driveway patching. Salisbury permits and inspections apply to this work, and we coordinate them so the repair is documented properly.

Questions to ask before any sewer repair

Ask for the camera footage, ask where the break is and how deep it is, and ask what each repair method will do to your yard. A good plumber answers all three plainly.

Ask also about warranties on the work and about what happens if the repair fails. We tell you what we stand behind before you decide.

Sewer or septic: which system is your home on?

Many homes in the Salisbury area connect to the Salisbury-Rowan Utilities sewer system, while homes farther out may use a septic tank and drain field. The difference changes who is responsible and what a repair looks like.

If a tank is overdue for pumping or the drain field is wet, the trouble sits on your property and no utility crew is involved. We can tell you whether the symptoms point to the sewer line, the tank or the field, and we say so plainly when the right next step is a septic pumping contractor.

Code check

Code and standards for sewer line repair

Sewer repairs that change pipe size, slope or routing are regulated under the North Carolina plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Permits and inspections apply, and an inspection confirms joints, bedding and slope before the trench is closed.
