Installation is different from a quick swap

A replacement puts a new heater where the old one stood. A new installation means deciding where the heater goes, how it is vented or powered, how water gets to it and where the drain and relief line terminate. Those decisions affect cost, safety and how well the system works for the next decade.

If your old unit has simply failed, our water heater replacement page covers that faster path.

How a planned installation runs

Site visit

We look at the proposed location, the supply lines and the electrical or gas service.

Written plan and price

You receive the equipment choice, installation scope and price before work starts.

Permit and install

We pull the permit, set the unit, connect and vent it, and test.

Inspection and handoff

We walk through the shutoffs, the relief valve and basic maintenance.

Where the heater goes matters in Salisbury homes

Salisbury has houses from nearly every period. Fulton Heights bungalows from the early 1900s, mid-century ranches and newer subdivisions all hide water heaters in different spots, from garages and closets to attics and crawl spaces. Each spot has its own clearance, drainage and venting needs.

In older homes we often find that the existing heater location was chosen decades ago for reasons that no longer apply. Moving it can improve access and shorten pipe runs, but it should be planned and permitted, not improvised.

What we confirm before installing

These checks prevent surprises on installation day.

  • Clearance around the unit and access for future service.
  • Combustion air and venting for gas units, or circuit capacity for electric ones.
  • A safe drain path for the pressure relief discharge pipe.
  • Whether a drain pan is needed to protect floors below.
  • An expansion tank if the plumbing has a closed system.
  • Permit requirements through Salisbury Development Services.

What you get when the installation is finished

You get a heater that is level, supported, connected with proper shutoffs and tested for leaks and temperature. The relief valve discharges to a safe location, and any vent or electrical connection is checked before we leave.

We also show you where the shutoffs are and how to drain a few gallons for maintenance. That five-minute walkthrough prevents most of the calls we get later.

Matching the new unit to your hot water use

A household of one or two uses far less hot water than a family of five, and the right unit differs. We calculate a first-hour rating or flow rate that fits the way you live.

Oversizing wastes energy and money. Undersizing leaves you with cold showers. A short conversation about your habits solves both.
