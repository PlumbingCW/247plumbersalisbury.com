Disposal jammed or leaking: safe steps first

Never put your hand into a disposal. Switch it off at the wall switch and, if you can, at the breaker before you do anything else.

If the unit is leaking under the sink, shut off the water to the sink and call (704) 951-1635.

  • Switch off the disposal and the breaker.
  • Use a flashlight to look, never your hand.
  • Close the angle stops if it is leaking.
  • Do not run it again until it has been checked.

What the symptoms mean

Humming, no spin

The impeller is jammed.

Nothing happens

The reset button has tripped or power is off.

Leaks from the bottom

Failed seals, usually a replacement.

Leaks from the top

A loose sink flange or seal.

Slow drain or backup

A clog in the drain or P-trap.

Bad smell

Food residue in the grind chamber.

Repair or replace

A jam can often be cleared by turning the impeller with a hex key from below and pressing the reset button. If the unit leaks from the body or the motor has failed, replacing it is cleaner and cheaper than repairing it.

A replacement usually takes less than two hours. We choose a motor size that suits your household and cooking habits.

Disposal habits that prevent most calls

Run cold water before, during and after use. Avoid putting grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, bones, pasta and fibrous vegetables into the unit, because these clog the line even if the blades handle them.

If the kitchen sink backs up, the problem may be in the drain line, so see drain cleaning. A new disposal can also be installed at the same time as a faucet replacement.

What a proper installation includes

We remove the old unit and the mounting ring, clean the sink flange, apply plumber's putty, seat the new mounting assembly and hang the disposal. We connect the drain, run the dishwasher line if present and connect power.

We then test for leaks, run water through the unit and check the dishwasher drain. A disposal that is only hand-tight at the flange leaks within weeks, so we torque it correctly.

Wiring matters. If your sink has no outlet or switch, we explain what is needed and who should perform the electrical work before you commit.

Why disposals jam and when the jam is a symptom

A single jam from a fruit pit or a spoon is nothing to worry about. Frequent jams point to an undersized motor, a worn impeller or a drain line that is not carrying waste away fast enough.

If your disposal jams repeatedly or the sink backs up behind it, ask for a drain check before you buy another unit.

Code check

Code and standards for garbage disposal

Disposal drains and dishwasher connections follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in North Carolina, including trap, vent and backflow rules.

Wiring for the disposal falls under the National Electrical Code (NEC), so a missing outlet or switch is an electrician's job.
