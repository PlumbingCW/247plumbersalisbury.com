Start here: the fast checks that save a service call

Before you call, look at the simple things. An electric heater with a tripped breaker or a gas unit with a pilot that blew out can be the whole problem. If a reset works once and fails again, something else is wrong and the repair is worth doing properly.

If you see water pooling, skip the checks and call. A leak from the tank shell is a water heater replacement job, while a leak from a fitting or valve is often repairable.

What to do if the heater is leaking or smells wrong

A leaking connection can often be stopped by closing the cold water valve above the heater. A gas smell is different. Leave the house, avoid switches and flames, and call the gas utility from outside before you call us for the repair.

For a hot-water leak or a loud pressure release, shut off power or gas to the heater and call (704) 951-1635 right away.

Common failures and what they usually mean

A technician finds the real cause before parts are replaced.

Not enough hot water

A failed lower element or thermostat on an electric unit, or a dip tube issue, shrinks the hot water you get.

No hot water at all

A tripped high-limit switch, a dead upper element, a failed thermostat or a gas valve problem.

Dripping relief valve

Either the valve has worn out or pressure is too high from a missing or waterlogged expansion tank.

Smelly or discolored water

Bacteria reacting to the anode rod or a tank interior that has started to corrode.

Noise

Sediment on the bottom. Flushing sometimes fixes it, and sometimes it means the tank is near the end.

Pilot keeps going out

A weak thermocouple, a draft or a failing gas valve.

Repair or replace: how we decide

We tell you what is wrong and what each choice costs. A modest repair on a heater in good shape is easy to recommend. A repair on a tank that is leaking, corroded or well past ten years rarely pays off, and we will say so.

Salisbury's soft water helps tanks last, because less scale means fewer burned elements. That is one reason an older heater here is sometimes worth repairing when the tank itself is sound. If the shell is leaking, replacement is the honest answer.

Parts and safety checks we do on every repair call

Whatever the original complaint, we check the temperature and pressure relief valve, the discharge pipe, the shutoff valves and the condition of the tank bottom. A repair that ignores these can leave a hidden safety problem behind.

We also test the water temperature at a nearby tap. Water that is too hot is a scald risk, and water that is too cool can encourage bacteria growth in the tank, so the right setting matters.

Code check

Code and standards for water heater repair

Repairs that touch the relief valve, discharge pipe, venting or gas and electrical connections are judged against the North Carolina State Building Code, whose plumbing section is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

That is why a repair visit includes a check of the safety devices, not just the part that failed.
