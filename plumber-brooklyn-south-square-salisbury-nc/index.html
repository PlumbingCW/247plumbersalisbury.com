Plumber in Brooklyn-South Square, Salisbury, NC

Brooklyn-South Square is a small historic district in Salisbury, centered on E Bank Street, with grand houses and modest cottages on the same blocks. This page covers plumbing help there, including questions for owners of rental houses.

A small district with a long history

The area was first used for industry. A cotton factory built in 1830 on the 200 block of E Bank Street was later used as a Confederate prison and was destroyed in 1865. Two brothers bought the prison area in 1866, and building on the land was slow after the war.

The National Register nomination counts 23 dwellings by 1882 and 75 by 1902, and the neighborhood was stable into the 1950s. The district covers about 15 acres, and the city designated it a local historic district in 1999.

Streets and edges

The nomination names these streets: S Lee, E Fisher, E Bank, S Long and S Shaver. E Bank Street is the focal point of the district.

It is bounded on the north by commercial development along E Innes Street and on the west by downtown's commercial area, and Southern Railway tracks run near its western edge. S Long Street was widened in the early 1980s, so it now cuts through the middle. The district sits inside the City of Salisbury.

What the history means for a plumbing call

Knowing a district's history does not show what is inside a particular wall. It does show that small lots here hold houses from very different periods, so a plumber should look at your house and not at the neighborhood's reputation.

Describe what you see when you call. Before you approve a large repair, ask what was found on camera and at the pressure check, and ask for the scope and the price in plain terms.

Steps to take before the plumber arrives

Steps that help in a house of any age.

  • Close the valve under the fixture first, and use the main if water is coming from a wall.
  • Do not mix drain chemicals. A line that backs up again needs a camera look, not another bottle.
  • Stop using fixtures that share the clogged drain.
  • Write down when the problem began and what changed that day.
  • Do not dig near a retaining wall to reach a pipe. Ask a plumber to locate the line first.

Terraced lots and drainage

The nomination notes stone or concrete block retaining walls on terraced lots. When any outdoor plumbing work is planned, ask the plumber how the work will reach the pipe without disturbing a wall.

Also look at where roof and yard water goes, because water that runs toward a foundation is a drainage question first. If you have a basement or crawlspace that collects water, see our sump pump service page for what a pump does and does not solve.

Owners and tenants

A note for owners of rental houses

If you rent out a house here, tell your tenant who to call when a plumbing problem starts and keep that instruction in writing. Small leaks and slow drains, which our drain cleaning page covers, cost more when nobody knows who is responsible.

If you are a tenant, check your lease and tell the owner or property manager before calling a plumber, because they may need to approve the work.
