Why pipes burst, and why the cause matters

Pipes burst from freezing, high pressure, corrosion and old fittings letting go. Freeze damage in North Carolina tends to follow cold snaps that catch homeowners off guard, and the bursts show up when the pipe thaws, not while it is frozen.

The cause shapes the fix. A split from freezing is a local repair. A burst from corrosion is a sign that the pipe near it is also weak, and we tell you honestly when the rest of the run should be replaced.

Where bursts happen most

Crawl spaces and attics

Unheated areas expose supply lines to freezing.

Exterior walls

Pipes on the cold side of insulation are exposed.

Hose bibs and outdoor lines

Connected hoses trap water that freezes.

Older galvanized or polybutylene

Aged materials fail under pressure.

Cracked pipes, drain pipes and copper repairs

Not every pipe failure is a dramatic burst. A hairline crack in a supply line, a pinhole in older copper or a broken drain pipe under the house can leak for weeks before anyone notices.

Copper can be repaired with a new soldered section or a push-fit coupling, and a damaged drain pipe is replaced with a new length. We cut back to sound pipe so the repair holds instead of failing a few inches away.

Code and standards for burst pipe repair

Replacing a burst section with approved pipe and fittings follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in the North Carolina plumbing code.

Pipe and fitting materials must be approved for potable water, which is one more reason not to improvise a repair.
